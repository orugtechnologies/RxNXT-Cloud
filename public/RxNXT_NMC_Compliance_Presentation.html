Regulatory Framework

Statutory Backbone in the Gazette (Pages 70–124)

The gazette is split into Hindi (P. 1–69) and English (P. 70–124). Telemedicine & messaging are governed under Chapter 2 & Guideline-11.

1

Tools for Telemedicine

Page 100, Sec 1.4 & 2.1

NMC classifies tools into Video, Audio, and Text. WhatsApp is explicitly cited: "General messaging/text/chat platforms (WhatsApp, Google Hangouts, Facebook Messenger etc.)".

2

Electronic ID Display

Page 104, Sec 1.7

"Every RMP shall display the registration number accorded by the State Medical Council or NMC on all prescriptions, website, electronic communications (WhatsApp/email etc.)".

3

Public vs Private Messaging

Page 82, Guideline-6

RMPs are barred from prescribing on public social media forums. However, private 1-to-1 encrypted transmission via WhatsApp directly to the consulting patient is fully sanctioned.

Regulation 29 (Page 73) & Telemedicine Practice Guidelines

Regulation 29 explicitly recognizes telemedicine consultations conducted in compliance with the Telemedicine Guidelines. Both the 2020 Guidelines and the 2023 Gazette maintain complete consistency regarding the legality of e-prescriptions transmitted via electronic channels.

Deep Dive: Prescriptions

Transmitting PDF Prescriptions via WhatsApp

Verification against Section 3.7.9 (Page 108) of the NMC Gazette Notification.

Direct Gazette Excerpt (Page 108)

"RMPs shall provide a clear photograph, scanned or digital copy of a duly signed prescription (e-Prescription) to the patient via email or over any other messaging platform with their full name, qualifications and registration number with the State medical council or the Indian Medical register clearly visible on the prescription. There is no need to take print out of the e-prescription."

Statutory Requirements for the PDF

  • ✓ RMP Identifiers: Full Name, Qualifications, Clinic/Hospital Address, Contact Info, and NMC/SMC Reg No.
  • ✓ Patient Details: Full Name, Age, Sex, Contact Details, and Date/Time/Place of consultation.
  • ✓ Generic Names: Prescribe using generic names in CAPITAL letters with formulation, dose, frequency, and duration.
  • ✓ Signature: Duly signed (scanned or authenticated digital signature) with official stamp.

Validity & Operational Rules

  • ✓ No Physical Printout: The digital PDF received via WhatsApp is legally sufficient for pharmacists to dispense medicines.
  • ✓ Validity Window: Valid for 2 weeks from issue or until dispensed once by the pharmacist.
  • ✓ Pharmacy Choice: Direct transfer to a third-party pharmacy cannot be automated without explicit patient consent.
  • ⚠ Prohibited Drugs: Cannot prescribe Schedule X drugs or NDPS narcotics over telemedicine/electronic prescriptions.
Deep Dive: Adherence & Reminders

Medication Reminders via WhatsApp

Enabling treatment adherence while maintaining statutory clinical boundaries.

⏱️

Continuity of Care

Page 109, Sec 1.3(c)

NMC mandates doctors to take proactive steps to promote continuity of care and ensure patients adhere to the treatment plan. Scheduled reminders directly serve this ethical objective.

📋

Patient Counseling

Page 106, Sec 3.7.3

Defines counseling as specific instructions on dos and don'ts, timely intake, and following through on the medical regimen prescribed during the consultation.

🔒

Doctor-Only Authority

Page 116, Sec 5.7

AI/tech platforms are strictly forbidden from prescribing or dispensing medical advice autonomously. Reminders must only relay the exact regimen authored by the RMP.

Guardrails for 100% WhatsApp Reminder Compliance

1.
Opt-in / Direct Number: Sent strictly to the patient's verified mobile number given during intake (implied consent for treatment management).
2.
Confidential 1-to-1 Channel: Never send health notifications in groups or shared family links without authorization (Reg. 24).
3.
Zero Commercial Solicitation: Never bundle pharmacy discount codes, commercial brand promotions, or clinic referral links (Reg. 7 & 10).
4.
Doctor Identification: Header or footer clearly identifying the consulting doctor and their registration number (Sec. 1.7).

Why RxNXT Avoids Aggregator Pitfalls

Understanding Section 5.2 (Page 116) and Regulation 7 (Page 71) on patient solicitation.

Aggregators (Practo, Lybrate, etc.)

Regulatory Risks

Open healthcare discovery marketplaces face severe compliance challenges under the 2023 Guidelines:

  • ✕ Doctor Ratings & Reviews: Section 5.2 explicitly bars doctors from participating in platforms with user reviews or star ratings.
  • ✕ Search Algorithm Promotions: Regulation 5 (P. 82) prohibits paying to be prioritized at top of doctor directories.
  • ✕ Patient Solicitation / Commission: Regulation 7 & 9 forbid fee splitting and soliciting patients via agents/lead-gen platforms.

RxNXT (Private Workflow SaaS)

Fully Safe

RxNXT functions purely as a clinical utility and engagement tool for the doctor's existing clinic practice:

  • ✓ No Public Directory: Not a consumer marketplace; does not host public ratings or commercial advertisements.
  • ✓ Established Relationship: Used strictly between the doctor and their own consulting patients (Implied Consent).
  • ✓ Pure Adherence Focus: Designed to bridge the gap between prescribing and treatment completion, fulfilling NMC continuity rules.
💡 Key Takeaway: By positioning itself as an EMR engagement layer rather than a doctor discovery marketplace, RxNXT provides doctors with safe, compliant technology infrastructure.

Conclusion

Regulatory Verdict & Current Legal Status

Final summary on RxNXT compliance and ongoing NMC developments.

The Verdict on RxNXT

RxNXT's core workflow—enabling doctors to generate digital prescriptions, transmit them over WhatsApp, and send automated medication schedule reminders—is completely compliant with the guidelines established by the National Medical Commission.

By avoiding the controversial aggregator business model (public star ratings, sponsored directory rankings, and commercial commission-splitting), RxNXT provides a compliant, legally sound infrastructure for modern medical clinics.

Note on the August 23, 2023 Abeyance

On August 23, 2023, the NMC placed the 2023 Conduct Regulations in temporary abeyance primarily due to disputes surrounding mandatory generic prescribing and pharma conference sponsorship restrictions.

Crucial Point: Even under the earlier regulations (2002 Code of Ethics + 2020 Telemedicine Practice Guidelines), WhatsApp transmission of digital prescriptions and treatment adherence communications remain 100% lawful.
